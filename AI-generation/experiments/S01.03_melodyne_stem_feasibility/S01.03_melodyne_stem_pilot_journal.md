## Session 1 — Run R02

Run ID: S01.03-R02-20260818  
Phase: 2-stem Melodyne feasibility probe (Lead Vocal + String Section)

### 1. Pre-Run Intent

- Stems under test: `08-finale-mm39-55-Lead-Vocal-16m.wav`, `08-finale-mm39-55-String-Section-16m.wav`
- SMF: 16-measure conductor track export from DP chunk
- Hypothesis: SMF + 2 aligned stems → useful bar-grid-aligned MIDI output; passing this check validates the alignment approach before committing all 9 stems.
- Algorithm assignments: Lead Vocal → Universal (duet/choral sections present; mixed polyphony likely); String Section → Polyphonic
- Key evaluation questions: (1) Do extracted MIDI events snap to the bar grid from the SMF? (2) Does Melodyne handle the vocal polyphony without collapsing to a single line? (3) Is the String Section extraction usably dense or overwhelmingly noisy?

- Output files: `08-finale-mm39-55-VoxStrOrch-raw-R02a.mid`; Melodyne project `S01.03-R02a-mm39-55-VoxStrOrch.melodyne`

### 3. Interpretation

- **SMF alignment (bar grid snap):** Material landed within beat parameters. Only minor manual adjustments required — no systematic offset or global drift detected.
- **Lead Vocal MIDI quality:** Vocal lines captured with strong melodic contour and expressive pitch detail. Spurious notes present in higher-than-expected quantity, attributable to performance expressivity (vibrato, portamento, microtonal inflection) generating extra blobs in Melodyne. These represent a cost of preserving expressive material rather than random noise — correctable in cleanup without losing compositionally significant content.
- **String Section MIDI quality:** Intonation, key centre, and most rhythmic values captured well. Articulation was imprecise — note boundaries and attack detail require cleanup. Acceptable given that cleanup was anticipated regardless; the harmonic and rhythmic skeleton is present and usable.
- **Unexpected anomalies:** None beyond the spurious vocal notes. No catastrophic failures, missing sections, or octave-register errors noted.

### 4. Comparative Insight

- **What worked:** Grid alignment via SMF tempo map; overall harmonic and rhythmic fidelity of both stems; vocal melodic contour preservation; string section key and rhythm capture.
- **What did not work:** Vocal spurious notes require cleanup pass; string articulation imprecise.
- **Go/no-go for full 9-stem R03:** **Go.** Process is validated. Cleanup burden is acceptable and expected.

### 5. Decisions

- Decision ID: S01.03-D-001
- Trigger evidence: R02a MIDI imported into DP; material aligns to grid with minor corrections; harmonic and melodic content usable.
- Chosen action: proceed to R03 full 9-stem extraction pass after quantization cleanup of R02a output.
- Retest condition: if R03 produces systematic failures on additional stems, revisit algorithm assignments before proceeding.

### 6. Run Close

- Main finding: Melodyne Studio with SMF tempo map produces bar-grid-aligned MIDI from Suno stems that is harmonically, structurally, and rhythmically coherent. Spurious notes from expressive vocal performance are the primary cleanup burden.
- Open blockers: quantization pass on R02a output pending; full evaluation requires studio monitoring (Scarlett + KH-80s).

## Session 1 — Run R03 (Full 9-Stem Extraction)

Run ID: S01.03-R03-20260818

### 1. Pre-Run Intent

- All 9 stems extracted in a single Melodyne session; quantized to 16th notes in DP.
- Hypothesis: full stem set will produce usable MIDI across most stems, with redundancy expected between the string-family stems.

### 2. Runs Executed (Reference)

- Run IDs: S01.03-R03-20260818
- Output: 9-stem MIDI imported into DP chunk "Suno Integration.03 preparation mm1-16 Melodyne MIDI Import Quantize"; quantized to 16th notes.

### 3. Interpretation

- **Overall correlation:** approximately 80–85% across all stems.
- **Metric precision:** fine; only minor adjustments required to align to grid.
- **Lead Vocal (file 1):** quite fine. Extracted all vocal parts. Limitation: in choral/ensemble contexts individual line isolation may be less effective.
- **String Section (file 2):** good rendition of the entire 16 measures; most complete harmonic coverage of the string family.
- **Violin (file 3):** weighted toward higher frequencies but includes lower string content at points. Together with Cello, produces a reasonable simulation of the complete string section.
- **Cello (file 4):** full individual evaluation pending studio session; see Violin above for combined string assessment.
- **Flute (file 5):** mostly wind chiffs and noise outside of actual pitch phonation. Some recognisable flute-like passages later in the piece (not yet assessed). Occasional oboe bleed. Stem is very sparse — consistent with the first 16 measures having little wind content in the original transformation.
- **Oboe (file 6):** more mechanical and artificial than Flute. Primarily captures sforzando tutti moments and scrapes pitch material; represents an aggregate subset of accented orchestral events rather than a clean oboe line.
- **Bassoon (file 7):** most disappointing wind extraction. Full assessment pending.
- **Double Bass (file 8):** quite good. Extracts a convincing bass-viol-like line. Some cello bleed at places; occasional orchestral artifacts at forte moments. Most useful of the lower-voice stems.
- **Viola (file 9 — last in extraction order):** not bad upon closer listening. Unable to extract a clean melodic line, but captures a middle-voice-dominated string stack. In sparser passages, melodic extraction improves noticeably.
- **String family redundancy (StgOrch, Violin, Viola):** all three provide a combined string aggregate rather than a clean instrument-separated line. Violin and Cello together adequately simulate the full string section; StgOrch and Viola add reinforcement with overlap.
- **Stem artifacts (clicks, radio-like fragments, stand sounds):** observed in several stems, particularly weaker ones. Inaudible in the summed stereo mix (unverified by null test — observation only), consistent with spectral masking. Structured latent components, not random separation noise; may have independent compositional value.
- **Hypothesis — iterative residual decomposition:** Viola (file 9, last) shows the most residual-character content, consistent with a cascade subtraction approach where each stem is extracted from the prior residual. Last stem receives accumulated subtraction error; sum-to-original is guaranteed by construction. Unverified — Beta feature; Suno has not published the implementation.

### 4. Comparative Insight

- **What worked:** vocal, string harmony skeleton, metric alignment, key centre fidelity.
- **What did not work:** wind stems unclear; double bass confused; string redundancy requires arbitration pass to deduplicate.
- **Go/no-go for full workflow:** **Go.** 80–85% correlation with 16th-note quantization is sufficient for notation-based continuation. Redundant stems are a feature as much as a problem — they provide harmonic reinforcement and cross-validation.

### 5. Decisions

- Decision ID: S01.03-D-002
- Trigger evidence: R03 full 9-stem import assessed in DP; metric and harmonic fidelity confirmed.
- Chosen action: proceed to cross-track arbitration and deduplication; prepare notation-ready MIDI for proof-of-use render.
- Retest condition: wind stems may benefit from a second pass with different Melodyne algorithm (Universal instead of Melodic); defer to R04 if needed.

### 6. Session Close

- Main finding: full 9-stem Melodyne extraction from Suno experimental stems produces a viable symbolic skeleton. Metric precision is strong; harmonic content is usable; wind and bass clarity are the primary limitations. Workflow is validated at pilot level.
- Open blockers: cross-track arbitration pending; proof-of-use render and full evaluation deferred to studio session with Scarlett + KH-80s.
- Next first action: cross-track arbitration and deduplication of string-family stems; proof-of-use render.
- Pilot gate status: **Go** — proceed to full workflow documentation and D01.04 spec.
